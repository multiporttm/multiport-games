Before a handmade sundial can tell true time, its latitude ring must be calibrated to the exact degree of the city it will stand in, tilting the whole instrument to match the sun's path overhead. Each round names a city and its target latitude in degrees, then the brass latitude ring starts dialed to some other reading entirely while a shadow needle slowly drifts away from true as the gauge sits exposed. Drag the ring with your mouse or finger, or nudge it with the Left and Right arrow keys, to dial the pointer onto the correct degree, then press Set Calibration or tap Space to lock it in before the drift carries the needle out of range. A precise calibration locks the ring true for that city and earns a strong bonus, a near match still works but loosely, and a badly drifted setting wastes a batch of calibration fluid. The target degrees grow more exacting and the needle drifts faster as more cities are calibrated, so a careful hand will be needed to complete the full atlas of latitudes before the fluid runs dry.
