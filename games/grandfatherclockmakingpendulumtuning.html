Once a grandfather clock's case and movement are built, the pendulum must be tuned so the clock keeps true time, and that work falls to a patient horological tuner. Each round, the dial posts a target beat rate and the pendulum swings at whatever rate its current length produces, shown on a tempo gauge. Drag the small rating nut up or down the pendulum rod with your mouse or finger, or press the Up and Down arrow keys, to shorten or lengthen the effective pendulum and bring the gauge needle into the green target zone. Once the tempo is close enough, watch the pendulum swing and tap Space or click Tap Beat exactly as it reaches the peak of each swing to lock the escapement into rhythm — timing your taps well earns a strong bonus, while sloppy timing or a badly mistuned length costs accuracy. Later rounds narrow the green zone and quicken the tempo, so a steady hand and a sharp ear for rhythm will keep the clock keeping perfect time.
