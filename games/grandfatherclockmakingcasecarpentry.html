Building a grandfather clock case begins at the sawbench, where a cabinetmaker turns rough oak planks into the precisely sized stiles, rails and panels that will form the case frame. Each round, a fresh plank slides onto the bench with a target length marked in chalk and the saw's sliding stop block starts off a random distance from it. Drag the stop block with your mouse or finger, or nudge it with the Left and Right arrow keys, until the saw blade sits as close to the chalk mark as you can manage, then press Cut Plank or tap Space to make the cut. A clean, accurate cut locks the plank into the rising case frame and earns a solid bonus, while a sloppy cut wastes part of your limited timber supply and only partially completes the frame. The chalk marks grow trickier and the stop block drifts faster as the case frame rises higher, so steady hands and a careful eye will see the cabinet through to its final, polished assembly before your timber runs out.
