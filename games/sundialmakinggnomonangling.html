Drag the gnomon blade (or use Left/Right arrows) so it swings onto the chalked target angle on the protractor arc, then press Lock Angle or hit Space before the hold timer empties. The closer you match the angle, the truer the pin holds — miss badly and the blade warps, wasting bronze.
Shaping a true sundial begins with angling the gnomon, the triangular blade whose edge must point exactly along the Earth's axis to cast an accurate shadow. Each round, a fresh bronze blade is set on the angling jig and a target angle is chalked onto the protractor arc behind it, while the blade itself starts pointed somewhere else entirely. Drag the blade with your mouse or finger, or nudge it with the Left and Right arrow keys, to swing its edge toward the chalk mark, then press Lock Angle or tap Space to drive the holding pin — but you only have a short hold window before the hammer falls on its own and locks whatever angle you are holding. A precise lock pins the blade true and earns a strong bonus toward the finished dial, while a sloppy lock warps the blade's edge and wastes part of your limited bronze stock. The target angles grow sharper and the hold window grows shorter as more gnomons are finished, so a steady hand and quick timing will see the full set of dials angled correctly before the bronze runs out.
